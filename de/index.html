Two things I am in the middle of

Neither is finished, and I am including them anyway. Work in flight tells you more about how someone thinks than work that has already been tidied into a case study.

In progress · not yet shipped

LEAP MCP: our platform, reachable from the customer's own AI assistant

Our customers were already leaving the application to get things done with AI. LEAP MCP is an MCP server that gives their assistant a direct, defined way into the platform, so the work happens with the product rather than around it.

Why

It is the harness idea pointed outward. Inside the team, agents became useful once they had the codebase's rules and a hard stop at review. For a customer, an assistant becomes useful once it has a defined surface into the product, carrying our permissions and our rules, instead of working around it.

The open question

What a customer should be able to let an assistant do on their behalf, and where the product has to stop and ask a person. Inside the team my answer was a hard stop at human review. For customers the line sits somewhere else, and I would rather draw it deliberately than discover it after an incident.

In progress · outcome not yet known

Moving from engineering leadership toward product engineering

Taking a request the whole way myself: understanding what the customer actually needs, shaping a solution, architecting it, building it, and deploying it to production. Deliberately not handing off at each boundary, because the handoffs are where the understanding leaks out.

Why

I have spent two years getting good at making a team deliver, and I have the evidence for it. What I have less evidence for is owning the entire line from a customer's problem to a running feature, holding the ambiguity myself instead of letting the organisation's structure absorb it. That is a different skill and I want to be able to prove it rather than assert it.

The open question

Success here is harder to measure than delivery. Shipping on time proves very little. The signal I am watching is whether what I ship changes what the customer actually does, and I am still working out how to observe that honestly rather than talking myself into it.

Why Germany, and how ready I am

The credential recognition is already done

The slowest part of hiring someone from outside the EU is usually credential recognition. Mine is finished and every document on my side is gathered. I am not waiting on paperwork. I am waiting on the right employer.

Work authorisation

  • EU Blue Card eligible. DAV University is listed in anabin with status H+, state-accredited.
  • My B.Tech is rated "corresponds" to a German Bachelor, so no individual Zeugnisbewertung is required. That is the step that usually adds months.
  • IT is a shortage occupation, which sets the applicable salary threshold.
  • You have a lever on the timeline if you want one. The accelerated skilled-worker procedure (§81a AufenthG) is filed by your legal team at your local Ausländerbehörde, under a power of attorney from me, for a €411 fee. It bundles the recognition and labour-approval steps into one pre-approval, and the consulate then decides in about three weeks. Happy to do my half of it.
  • Nothing on my side is outstanding. Every document is gathered. The only remaining step after a signed contract is the visa appointment.

Languages

English is my working language and has been for four years across US-facing product teams. German is a work in progress and I am not going to overstate it.

Practical

  • Start date: two months if you want it to be, about three if you leave it to the standard route. I serve 60 days' notice, two months, and the visa runs alongside it rather than after it — they do not add up. The only variable is the appointment queue at the German mission in India, which runs eight to twelve weeks in Delhi on the normal route and is what pushes the default out to three months. The accelerated procedure above is the way round it: a pre-approval, a fast-track appointment category, and a decision in about three weeks, which lands the visa inside my notice period. Two months is yours to unlock. Three is what I will commit to without it, because I would rather under-promise a start date than miss one you have planned headcount around.
  • Salary expectation: €70,000 to €90,000 for a senior individual-contributor role in Berlin, Munich a step above. For Engineering Manager or Lead scope, correspondingly higher. Benchmarked against the German market for the level, not against what I currently earn.
  • Targeting Berlin, Munich and Hamburg. Open elsewhere for the right team.
  • Comfortable fully remote, hybrid or onsite. I work CET-friendly hours already.
  • Open to senior and staff IC roles, not just management. I want my first year inside German engineering culture as an engineer. That is a deliberate choice, not a fallback.

What you would actually get

My first 90 days

Everything above is a method applied to problems I already understood. This is the same method pointed at a codebase and a team I do not know yet, which is the only version that is relevant to you. It is a plan, not a promise, and the first thing I would do is let you correct it.

Days 1 to 30

Count it myself, and ship something small

Read the last six months of incidents and post-mortems before I read the roadmap, because that is where the real constraints are written down. Sit in on support. Get the environment running from the README and fix the README where it lied to me, which is the cheapest useful first commit anyone can make. One-to-ones with every engineer, with one question I actually care about: what do you work around every week that nobody has written down?

By day 30: a small change of my own in production, and a written list of what I found that nobody told me.

Days 31 to 60

Write the reframe and let you attack it

One document, one sentence at the top naming what I think the actual problem is, with the options I ruled out and why sitting beside the one I am recommending. I would rather be wrong in writing in month two than vaguely right in conversation for a year. Then I take it to the people most likely to disagree and ask them to break it, the same way I escalated the challenge against my own benchmark in case 03.

By day 60: a proposal with its own falsification test attached.

Days 61 to 90

Fix one thing that depends on someone remembering

Pick the highest-consequence control that currently runs on human memory and replace it with something machine-checkable. Not a large migration; the smallest change that removes a recurring class of failure. In parallel, hand one thing I am currently the only person able to do to someone else, on purpose, because that is the habit that makes the second year work.

By day 90: one class of failure that can no longer happen quietly, and one thing I used to own that somebody else now does.
