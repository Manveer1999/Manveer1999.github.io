Two things I am in the middle of

Neither is finished, and I am including them anyway. Work in flight tells you more about how someone thinks than work that has already been tidied into a case study. For both of these I can tell you what I am trying and why. I cannot yet tell you whether it worked.

In progress · outcome not yet known

A project-specific harness that carries a request end to end

I am building a harness that takes a development request and carries it through to completion, with the quality gates checked at each step rather than bolted on at the end. Not a general-purpose agent. Something that knows one codebase: its conventions, its tests, its release rules, and that refuses to hand back work which has not passed them.

Why

The agent I already run stops at "ready for human code review". That guardrail is right, and it also means a person is the bottleneck for everything after that point. The question I am actually chasing is how much of the path from request to production can be made gate-checked rather than supervised, without quietly lowering the bar for what counts as done.

The open question

Speed is the obvious measure and probably the wrong one. The measure I keep circling back to is whether the gates catch what a reviewer would have caught, which means I first need a baseline of what our reviewers actually catch. I do not have that baseline yet, so I cannot honestly claim this is working.

In progress · outcome not yet known

Moving from engineering leadership toward product engineering

Taking a request the whole way myself: understanding what the customer actually needs, shaping a solution, architecting it, building it, and deploying it to production. Deliberately not handing off at each boundary, because the handoffs are where the understanding leaks out.

Why

I have spent two years getting good at making a team deliver, and I have the evidence for it. What I have less evidence for is owning the entire line from a customer's problem to a running feature, holding the ambiguity myself instead of letting the organisation's structure absorb it. That is a different skill and I want to be able to prove it rather than assert it.

The open question

Success here is harder to measure than delivery. Shipping on time proves very little. The signal I am watching is whether what I ship changes what the customer actually does, and I am still working out how to observe that honestly rather than talking myself into it.
